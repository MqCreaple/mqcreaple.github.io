## State Space A mathematical trick to turn a second-order ODE into two first-order ODEs: $$ \ddot{\theta} = \frac{\tau_\text{ext}}{ml^2} - \frac{g}{l}\sin(\theta) \quad \rightarrow \quad \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{bmatrix}\theta \newline \dot{\theta}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\dot{\theta} \newline \frac{\tau_\text{ext}}{ml^2} - \frac{g}{l}\sin(\theta) \end{bmatrix} $$ The space of vectors $\begin{bmatrix}\theta & \dot{\theta}\end{bmatrix}^T$ is called the **state space**, which includes all physically distinct states of the system. An equivalent definition uses generalised coordinates and conjugate momenta (in this case the angle $\theta$ and angular momentum $L$) as the state variables. For an unconstrained mechanical system described by $n$ second-order ODEs, its state has $2n$ elements.
